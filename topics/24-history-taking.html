📋 History Taking + Clinical Measurements

Foundation skill for all NSW PATH consultations · Document in MedAdvisor · Share with GP · Records ≥7 years (or to age 25 if patient <18)
💡 Structured history-taking + accurate vital signs are the foundation of safe pharmacist prescribing. Open question first, listen actively ~90 sec, then targeted questioning. SOAP for documentation; SOCRATES for pain history.

📋 SOAP framework

LetterElement
SubjectiveWhat the patient experiences and tells you
ObjectiveWhat you discover when you examine the patient
AssessmentPutting it all together (impression, differentials)
PlanTreatment, referral, follow-up, safety-net

📋 Seven critical elements of clinical history

  1. Presenting problem / chief complaint (in patient's own words)
  2. History of the present illness (HPI)
  3. Past history of diseases + surgery
  4. Medications + allergies
  5. Family history
  6. Social history
  7. Systems review

📋 Additional history items

Associated symptoms

  • Chest pain + N/V + diaphoresis (Acute Coronary Syndrome — ACS)
  • Headache + photophobia (meningitis, migraine)
  • Vomiting + visual disturbance (raised intracranial pressure)

Menstrual + reproductive history (CRITICAL — for abdo pain, N/V, women of childbearing age)

⛔ Always ask: "Could you be pregnant now?" — even if patient says "no chance".
  • Date of Last Normal Menstrual Period (LNMP)
  • Gravida (number of pregnancies)
  • Para (number of births >20 weeks' gestation)
  • Contraception method
  • Previous obstetric / gynaecological history (D&C, ectopic, miscarriage)

Sexual history (for vaginal/urethral discharge, genital/testicular pain, lumps, sores, UTI in young patient, hyperacute conjunctivitis)

  • New / multiple partners (specifically last 60 days)
  • Condom use
  • Previous STIs
  • Partner recently treated for STI
  • Sex worker contact
  • Same-sex partners

Often forgotten — remember to ask about:
  • Anticoagulants
  • OCP / hormonal contraception
  • Patches (HRT, fentanyl, nicotine)
  • Implants (etonogestrel, levonorgestrel IUD)
  • Injections (DMPA, monthly biologics)
  • Inhalers (often forgotten in "what do you take")

📋 Past history

  • Illnesses (esp. cardiac, respiratory, diabetes, cancer, psychiatric, autoimmune)
  • Surgical procedures + dates
  • Implants / prostheses
  • Significant childhood illnesses
  • Smoking + alcohol (record here)

👪 Family history (parents, siblings, children, grandparents)

  • Ischaemic heart disease, especially at an early age (<55 male, <65 female)
  • Strokes (early age)
  • Malignancy (breast, colorectal, ovarian, prostate, melanoma)
  • Mental illness
  • Inheritable diseases (haemochromatosis, BRCA, polyposis)

🌍 Social history

  • Occupation (exposures, ergonomic factors)
  • Education
  • Migration / overseas travel (parasitic, TB, hepatitis exposure)
  • Immunisations
  • Social supports (lives with whom, carer responsibilities, financial)
  • Diet · exercise
  • Cultural factors (esp. Aboriginal + Torres Strait Islander status — ARF/RHD risk)
  • Driving / occupation-relevant function

📋 Systems review principles

  • Targeted — relate questioning to presenting problem + HPI + past history
  • Note relevant negatives
  • Note "red flags" explicitly
  • Don't ask every symptom in every system if not relevant

🗣️ Opening the consultation (greeting + introduction)

  1. Hand hygiene + identify self (name, role)
  2. Confirm patient's identity (2 identifiers — name + DOB)
  3. Explain purpose + estimated time
  4. Obtain consent (verbal documented)
  5. Ensure privacy (consult room, door, no eavesdropping)
  6. Open question: "What brings you in today?"
  7. Listen actively without interrupting (~90 seconds typically)

🎭 Calgary-Cambridge stages (communication framework)

  1. Initiating the session
  2. Gathering information (open → closed funnel)
  3. Building the relationship (empathy, support)
  4. Providing structure (signposting, summary)
  5. Explanation + planning (chunking + checking understanding)
  6. Closing the session

🚨 Red flag screening (mandatory at end of HPI)

Verbalise the relevant red-flag screen for the presenting complaint:

"I'd like to check for some serious things we want to rule out..."

Then list and ask about each in patient-friendly language.

📋 Documentation — NSW Authority requirements

Every consultation record must contain:
  • Sufficient information to identify the patient
  • Date of consultation
  • Name of pharmacist who undertook consultation
  • Relevant clinical history + examination
  • Clinical opinion reached
  • Actions taken (including non-prescribing rationale)
  • Particulars of any medication supplied (form, strength, amount)
  • Advice / information given to patient
  • Any consent given by patient
  • Stored securely ≥7 years (or until age 25 if patient was <18)

📋 Sharing with GP (NSW Authority requirement)

  • Share record with patient's usual treating medical practitioner (with consent)
  • Upload to My Health Record unless patient declines
  • If patient doesn't have a GP → HealthDirect 1800 022 222

🎯 OSCE pearls

  • Open question first: "Tell me what's been happening"
  • Listen without interrupting (~90 sec)
  • Signpost transitions: "I'd like to ask about your medications next..."
  • Summarise back to patient: "So to make sure I've got this right..."
  • SOCRATES narrated through HPI
  • LMP + pregnancy possibility for women of childbearing age — ALWAYS
  • Allergy characterisation (rash vs anaphylaxis)
  • Red flag screen verbalised explicitly
  • Vital signs measurement narrated with values + interpretation
  • 2-identifier check + consent + privacy

⚠️ Pitfalls

  • Closed questions too early ("Is it severe?" — get severity description first)
  • Forgetting LMP / "could you be pregnant"
  • Allergy as "I'm allergic to penicillin" without asking what happened
  • Missing OTC / herbal / recreational meds
  • Not stating relevant negatives in summary
  • Forgetting to ask family history for cardiac / cancer
  • Skipping social history (occupation = exposure clues)
  • Forgetting to document non-prescribing reasoning with same rigour as prescribing
