👂 Acute Otitis Media (AOM) + Externa (AOE)

NSW planned/pilot services · Patient ≥2 years · Otoscopy required for AOM (if TM not visible → must not prescribe)
💡 >80% of AOM is self-limiting (resolves in 2–3 days). Antibiotics reserved for selected higher-risk patients. AOE = "swimmer's ear" — topical only; NO oral antibiotics for AOE under pharmacist scope.

🟢 Acute Otitis Media (AOM) — middle ear

Diagnosis criteria

⛔ "A red tympanic membrane (TM) or pain alone is NOT sufficient to diagnose AOM." Requires rapid onset (within 48h) of: pain + signs of TM inflammation (bulging, opaque, red or white from pus) + signs of middle ear effusion. If TM not visible, do NOT prescribe.

✅ Eligibility

  • Patient ≥2 years
  • TM visible on otoscopy
  • Diagnosis criteria met (above)
  • No RED flags below

🔴 RED — DO NOT treat → ED

Trigger
Uncertain diagnosis · TM not visible
Recent significant ear or head trauma (hearing loss / balance disturbance)
Severe / rapidly worsening: severe pain, headache, facial paralysis, balance disturbance, confusion, focal neuro signs, red/swollen/tender behind ear (mastoiditis)
Sepsis signs: fever/hypothermia, respiratory distress, tachycardia, rash, hypotension, cold extremities, dehydration, marked irritability/lethargy
TM red flag findings: crust/scab/granulation in attic region; severely retracted TM / retraction pocket in attic; dull white mass behind TM / chalky patches; perforation in attic/edge or medium-to-large perforation
Persistent TM perforation >6 weeks after initial presentation

🟠 ORANGE — TREAT + concurrently refer

Treat AOM AND refer at same consult.
TriggerAction
Otorrhoea (otic discharge) presentTreat + refer
Children with signs of complications OR high-risk for complicationsTreat + refer
No improvement / worsening after 48h analgesia + supportive careTreat + refer
Recurrent: ≥3 episodes in 6 mo OR ≥4 in 12 moTreat + refer (consider grommets)
Persistent Otitis Media with Effusion (OME) ("glue ear") — >3 moTreat + refer
High-risk groups: Aboriginal + Torres Strait Islander children (remote, <2 yo, first episode <6 mo, FHx CSOM, current/prior TM perforation); all children with immunocompromise / cochlear implant / craniofacial abnormality (incl. cleft palate) / Down syndrome / severe visual impairment / hearing loss.

💊 First-line antibiotics (when indicated)

ScenarioDrugDose & Duration
No pen allergyAmoxicillin ✅15 mg/kg (max 500 mg) PO q8h × 5 days
OR 30 mg/kg (max 1 g) PO q12h × 5 days (better adherence)
Pen allergy — non-severeCefuroxime15 mg/kg (max 500 mg) PO q12h × 5 days
Pen allergy — severeTrimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole4+20 mg/kg (max 160+800 mg) PO q12h × 5 days
⚠️ Cefuroxime preferred over cefalexin/cefaclor — superior S. pneumoniae activity. Decongestants NOT recommended for AOM (no evidence).

💊 Symptomatic (all AOM)

  • Paracetamol 15 mg/kg q4–6h prn — preferred first-line
  • Ibuprofen if ≥3 months and no contraindication
  • Warm compress

🔵 Acute Otitis Externa (AOE) — "swimmer's ear"

✅ Eligibility

  • Patient ≥2 years
  • Both required: symptoms (ear pain often severe, itch/fullness ± hearing loss/jaw pain) + signs (tragus/pinna tenderness OR diffuse canal oedema/erythema ± otorrhoea, regional lymphadenitis)
  • No RED flags below

🔴 RED — DO NOT treat → refer

Trigger
Uncertain diagnosis
Complete occlusion of ear canal (cannot instil drops)
Suspected mastoiditis
Systemic symptoms (fever, weakness, irritability, ↓ appetite)
Recent significant head / ear trauma
High-risk comorbidities: immunocompromise, diabetes (malignant otitis externa risk)
TM red flags (as for AOM)
Infection extending beyond canal (pinna inflammation, folliculitis)
Inadequate response to appropriate Rx
Recurrent: ≥3 in 6 mo OR ≥4 in 12 mo

🟠 ORANGE — TREAT + concurrently refer

TriggerAction
Severe symptoms (intense pain) — PROVIDED no complete canal occlusionTreat + refer at same consult

💊 Ear drop technique (counsel)

  • Warm bottle in hand · tilt head opposite side, ear up
  • Pull pinna up + back (adult) / down + back (child)
  • Instill drops; stay tilted 3–5 min; hand hygiene
  • Avoid ear syringing (can worsen) · keep dry during course + 2 weeks after (earplug or cotton + petroleum jelly for showers)

📋 Bacterial vs Fungal AOE — quick distinguishing

FeatureBacterialFungal (otomycosis)
PainSevere (worse with pinna pull / tragus pressure)Itch dominant, mild pain
CanalNarrow, swollenUsually wide
DischargeSmelly creamy/purulent yellowThick "wet newspaper" debris, hyphae, "salt + pepper"

🎯 OSCE pearls (both)

  • Distinguish AOM (middle ear, viral often, antibiotic not always) vs AOE (outer ear, swimmer's ear)
  • Age ≥2 yo for both; otoscopy + TM visibility mandatory for AOM
  • Pull pinna correctly (adult vs child)
  • Always ask about grommets / TM perforation (most drops contraindicated)
  • Diabetes / immunocompromise = malignant OE risk → REFER
  • Counsel keep ear dry: course duration + 2 weeks post

💎 Pitfalls

  • AOM in adults is rare — consider URTI with referred pain, TMJ, malignancy
  • AOE pain on tragus = pathognomonic; AOM pain on tragus usually absent
  • Otomycosis often follows antibiotic drops → clotrimazole drops (refer)
  • Cotton buds = the enemy (counsel against)
