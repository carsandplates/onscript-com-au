🩸 Blood Glucose Level (BGL) Targets

Quick reference across guidelines · Pharmacist assesses BGL in screening, N/V workup (DKA risk), unwell patients on SGLT2/insulin/sulfonylurea, MAS/Webster reviews, vaccinations (defer if unwell) · See also 12-nausea-vomiting + 24-history-taking
💡 Pregnancy targets are tighter than non-pregnant (reduces macrosomia, neonatal hypoglycaemia, pre-eclampsia). SGLT2 inhibitor + unwell + N/V = euglycaemic DKA possible — BGL may be normal in DKA. This is the highest-yield pharmacist clinical pitfall.

📊 Normal ranges (general non-pregnant adults)

MeasureNormal
Fasting~4–6 mmol/L
Post-prandial (2 h after eating)Usually <7.8–8 mmol/L
NDSS pattern guidance; no strict universal post-prandial target for non-diabetic adults.

🔴 RED — BGL emergencies → ED

TriggerAction
BGL <3.0 mmol/L with symptoms (severe hypo)Give fast-acting carb (15 g) if conscious; repeat 15 min; complex carb after; if unconscious / cannot swallow → IM glucagon if available + 000
BGL >25 mmol/L with symptoms (hyperglycaemic crisis)DKA / HHS suspicion → ED
Any BGL + DKA featuresN/V, abdominal pain, fruity breath (ketones), Kussmaul breathing, dehydration, altered mental state → ED
Patient on SGLT2 inhibitor + unwell + N/V + abdominal painEuglycaemic DKA risk — BGL may be NORMAL but patient in DKA → ED
Hypoglycaemia in patient unable to take oral carb (cognitively impaired, vomiting, unconscious)000

🍬 Hypoglycaemia — recognition + management

Symptoms (autonomic + neuroglycopenic)

CategorySymptoms
Autonomic (early, ↓ BGL)Tremor, sweating, palpitations, hunger, anxiety, pallor
Neuroglycopenic (more severe)Confusion, drowsiness, slurred speech, weakness, blurred vision, headache, behavioural change
SevereSeizure, coma, focal neurological signs

Rule of 15 (conscious adult, BGL <4.0)

  1. Give 15 g fast-acting glucose (3 tsp sugar in water; 5–7 jellybeans; 150 mL juice; glucose tablets)
  2. Wait 15 minutes, recheck BGL
  3. Repeat if still <4.0
  4. Once BGL normalised → complex carb (sandwich, banana) to prevent recurrence
  5. Document + refer for medication review if on insulin/sulfonylurea

Unconscious or unable to swallow

  • IM glucagon 1 mg (GlucaGen HypoKit) if available — onset 10 min
  • Call 000
  • Lie on side (recovery position)
  • Do NOT give oral anything

🚨 Hyperglycaemic emergencies

Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) — usually T1DM but euglycaemic DKA possible with SGLT2 inhibitor

Features: N/V, abdominal pain, polyuria, polydipsia, dehydration, fruity (acetone) breath, Kussmaul breathing (deep + rapid), altered mental state. Check urine/blood ketones if available.
BGL: usually >14 mmol/L (may be LOWER in euglycaemic DKA with SGLT2 — KEY pitfall)
→ ED IMMEDIATELY

Hyperosmolar Hyperglycaemic State (HHS) — usually T2DM elderly

Features: severe dehydration, very high BGL (often >33 mmol/L), altered mental state, no significant ketosis, may have focal neuro signs
→ ED IMMEDIATELY

🤒 Sick day rules (counsel patients with diabetes)

  • Monitor BGL more frequently (q2–4h) when unwell
  • Don't stop insulin even if not eating (T1DM)
  • Check ketones if BGL persistently >15 mmol/L or unwell
  • Maintain fluids + carbohydrate intake if possible
  • SGLT2 inhibitors: withhold during acute illness (vomiting, dehydration, surgery, fasting) — DKA risk even with normal BGL
  • Metformin: withhold if dehydrated / acutely unwell (lactic acidosis risk)
  • Seek medical advice if vomiting >2h, persistent BGL >15 or <4, ketones present, drowsy

💊 Drugs that affect BGL (key for pharmacist review)

Cause hyperglycaemia

  • Corticosteroids (esp. prednisolone)
  • Atypical antipsychotics (olanzapine, clozapine, quetiapine)
  • Thiazide diuretics (high dose)
  • β-blockers (mask hypo symptoms + ↑ BGL slightly)
  • Tacrolimus, ciclosporin
  • Protease inhibitors
  • Statins (modest)

Cause hypoglycaemia

  • Insulin
  • Sulfonylureas (glibenclamide > gliclazide, glimepiride)
  • Meglitinides (repaglinide)
  • Quinolones (moxifloxacin > ciprofloxacin)
  • Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole (esp. with sulfonylureas — potentiation)
  • Alcohol (esp. fasting)
  • Tramadol (rare)
  • Pentamidine

📏 BGL measurement technique (counsel patient)

  1. Hand hygiene; warm hands; clean finger with soap+water (alcohol swab dries skin)
  2. Insert strip; calibrate code if needed
  3. Lance side of fingertip (less painful than pad); rotate fingers
  4. Discard first drop; apply 2nd drop to strip
  5. Read result; document with time + relation to meal
  6. Dispose lancet in sharps container

Sources of error

  • Wet finger (dilutes sample)
  • Squeezing finger excessively (interstitial fluid dilution)
  • Cold extremities (poor perfusion)
  • Expired strips
  • Wrong calibration code
  • Anaemia (falsely high BGL)
  • Polycythaemia (falsely low)

🎯 OSCE pearls

  • Always check BGL in any unwell diabetic patient
  • SGLT2 + N/V + abdominal pain = DKA workup (euglycaemic DKA possible) → ED
  • Rule of 15 for symptomatic hypo (15 g carb → wait 15 min → recheck)
  • Different targets for different populations — pregnancy is tightest (ADA: <5.3 fasting, <6.7 2h pp)
  • Sick day rules for any diabetic with acute illness
  • Glucagon technique if patient prescribed (GlucaGen HypoKit reconstitute + IM)

💎 Pearls

  • HbA1c ≥6.5% = diabetes (confirmed with second test); 5.7–6.4% = prediabetes (ADA); RACGP uses ≥48 mmol/mol
  • HbA1c reflects ~3 months glycaemic average (RBC lifespan)
  • Pregnancy BGL targets are tighter — reduces macrosomia, neonatal hypo, pre-eclampsia
  • Bariatric surgery patients can have severe postprandial hypoglycaemia (dumping syndrome) — refer
  • Fingerprick vs venous lab BGL: capillary tends 5–10% higher post-meal; clinically equivalent for monitoring
  • Continuous Glucose Monitor (CGM) users: Time In Range (TIR) 70–180 mg/dL (3.9–10 mmol/L) target ≥70% — newer ADA metric
