🩺 Impetigo

NSW PATH-DERM permanent service · Adult: localised AND extensive in scope · Paediatric oral antibiotic = refer
💡 Superficial bacterial skin infection (S. aureus ± S. pyogenes). Non-bullous (~70%) = honey-coloured crusted lesions around mouth/nose. Bullous = flaccid blisters from S. aureus toxin. Highly contagious — exclude from school/childcare until 24h after starting antibiotic OR all lesions crusted.

✅ Eligibility (NSW scope)

  • Adults: localised (≤2 sores) AND extensive (>2 sores) — BOTH in scope
  • Paediatric: localised only (topical mupirocin) — refer if oral antibiotic needed
  • Otherwise well; no systemic features
  • No RED flags

🔴 RED — DO NOT treat → refer

⛔ Severe + widespread (with systemic features) is RED. Note: extensive (>2 sores) alone in a well adult is NOT a red flag — that's in scope.

🗣️ Counselling (must cover)

  • School/childcare exclusion until 24h after starting antibiotic OR all lesions crusted
  • Highly contagious — hand hygiene critical; don't share towels/clothing/bedding; trim fingernails
  • Wash skin daily with soap + water; keep lesions covered if possible
  • Mupirocin technique: clean + dry area, gently lift crusts with saline-soaked gauze, thin layer TDS × 5 days
  • Return if: spread, fever, swollen glands, deeper / very painful lesions (?cellulitis/ecthyma), eye involvement, no improvement after 5 days mupirocin

📋 Documentation

SOAP with explicit rationale (esp. narrow-spectrum choice). Safety-net advice (fever, rapid spread, worsening pain, systemic symptoms). Share with GP; retain ≥7 years (or to age 25 if patient <18). For ≥65 yo: document the mandatory concurrent referral.

🎯 OSCE pearls

  • State the scope clearly: adults — localised AND extensive in scope; paediatric oral = refer; severe + widespread = refer
  • Inspect + count lesions; check for honey crust + erythematous base
  • Screen systemic features explicitly
  • Ask about household contacts ("kids with yellow rashes" → contagion path)
  • Counsel exclusion period
  • Mention APSGN/ARF risk in ATSI / vulnerable populations (lower threshold for systemic Rx)
  • ≥65 yo: state treat + mandatory simultaneous referral aloud
  • Narrow spectrum first — verbalise diclox/flucloxacillin choice over cefalexin (AMS)

⚠️ Pitfalls

  • Don't over-refer extensive impetigo in adults — >2 sores in a well adult IS within scope
  • Don't default to cefalexin — narrow spectrum first
  • "Severe + widespread" ≠ "extensive": extensive means >2 sores (treat); severe means systemic features / cellulitis / sepsis (refer)
  • Neonates / <3 mo with impetigo = REFER (mimics SSSS)
  • Adult with "yellow crusts" after grandchild contact = think impetigo, not shingles
