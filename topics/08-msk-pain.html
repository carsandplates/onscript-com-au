🦴 Acute Musculoskeletal (MSK) Pain

NSW permanent service · Patient ≥18 yo · Mild, acute mechanical pain only · NSW Approved Medicines (oral NSAIDs) dated 25 February 2026
💡 NSAID selection is guided by safety profile (GI, CV, renal risk), not by analgesic potency. Use the shortest effective course; combine with non-pharmacological measures. Plan a taper / stop point at the start.
⚠️ No "treat-and-refer" pathway exists for this service. If the patient meets any red flag or referral criterion, they are NOT eligible under the Authority → refer (no supply). The two tiers below differ only in urgency of referral (ED/urgent vs GP) — not in whether you treat.

✅ Eligibility (all must be true)

  • Patient ≥18 years (for patients >50 yo, consider ↑ risk of serious underlying pathology — malignancy, minimal-trauma fracture)
  • Acute mechanical MSK pain (tissue/joint/vertebrae/muscle damage from trauma, postural strain, overuse, immobilisation)
  • Mild intensity (moderate–severe = refer)
  • Self-limiting expected (acute = <3 months)
  • No red flags or referral criteria below; consent obtained; patient present

🔴 RED FLAG warning signs → immediate referral to a medical practitioner (ED for time-critical)

Verbatim from the Practice Standard. Not exhaustive — apply clinical judgement. Any one = refer, no supply.
Red flag warning sign
Acute swelling, erythema and significant ↓ range of motion of a joint
Headache and acute onset of visual or auditory disturbances
Digital ischaemia or other signs of neurovascular compromise
Suspected nerve damage / mononeuropathy (loss of feeling, weakness, pain/burning, paraesthesia)
Neurological signs, including altered bladder or bowel function
Other non-MSK systemic symptoms suggestive of serious pathology (fever, malaise, weight loss, nausea, vomiting)
Systemically unwell (fever, tachycardia, hypotension)
Recent intravenous drug use
Risk of minimal-trauma fracture (osteoporosis, history of cancer)
Severe mechanism of injury · new limb deformity · new onset of skin rash
Unilateral calf tenderness with no history of recent trauma
Pitting oedema

🟠 Other referral criteria → not eligible, refer to GP (no supply)

Verbatim from the Practice Standard. These are exclusion criteria — meeting any means refer, no pharmacist supply (not "treat and refer").
  • Patient has any red flag warning sign (above)
  • Patient is aged under 18 years
  • Pain cannot be attributed to acute mechanical MSK pain (e.g. chronic or inflammatory pain)
  • History of chronic inflammatory MSK pain or complex underlying rheumatological conditions (including acute flares)
  • Swelling, redness, tenderness and warmth around a joint or within a muscle, or other alerting features that may indicate serious pathology
  • Non-MSK systemic symptoms that may indicate underlying pathology (fever, malaise, weight loss)
  • Patient is at high risk of acute rheumatic fever
  • Patient rates pain as moderate to severe, or has significant functional limitations due to pain
  • Patient is intoxicated due to drugs and/or alcohol
  • Patient is unable to accurately rate their pain (cognitive impairment or special needs)
  • Inadequate response to optimal treatment, or pain/other symptoms worsen; pain management inadequate
  • Patient requests an opioid, or is opioid-tolerant / recovering from an opioid use disorder

📎 Supplementary recognition aids (eTG / background — NOT in the NSW Practice Standard)

Serious conditions to keep in mind: septic arthritis · giant cell arteritis (sight-threatening; >70 yo, new headache, jaw claudication, visual sx) · uveitis/scleritis · acute myelopathy · cauda equina (back pain + bladder/bowel + reduced perianal sensation) · systemic vasculitis.

TUNAFISH (back-pain red flags): Trauma · Unexplained weight loss · Neurological deficit · Age <20 or >50 new · Fever / IVDU / immunocompromised · Intractable night pain · Steroid long-term / osteoporosis · Hx of cancer.

Acute rheumatic fever — high-risk groups (eTG): Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander, Māori or Pacific peoples in disadvantaged settings · personal hx ARF/RHD · household member with recent ARF/RHD · overcrowding + low SES · frequent travel to high-ARF settings. (The Practice Standard states only "high risk of acute rheumatic fever" — no age cut-off.)

⚠️ NSAID GI / CV / renal / pregnancy cautions

  • GI toxicity risk factors: age >65 · hx upper GI bleed · hx PUD · H. pylori · anticoagulants > antiplatelets > SSRIs/SNRIs > corticosteroids · smoking
  • Renal: avoid eGFR <60 · "triple whammy" (NSAID + ACEi/ARB + diuretic) = AKI risk
  • CV: avoid in heart failure, severe HTN, recent MI
  • Asthma: ~10–15% NSAID-sensitive — caution + monitor
  • Pregnancy — NSAIDs not appropriate, avoid: contraindicated from 20 weeks gestation and in the 3rd trimester (premature ductus arteriosus closure, oligohydramnios, fetal renal impairment, delayed/prolonged labour); also avoid in 1st & 2nd trimesters (specialist advice only; early use linked to ↑ miscarriage risk). TGA Category C. Pregnant patient needing analgesia → paracetamol / non-pharm; refer if inadequate.
  • Lactation: short-acting NSAIDs (e.g. ibuprofen) generally considered compatible; check AMH/eTG for the agent.

💊 Stepwise treatment (mild, acute MSK)

StepAction
1 — AlwaysReassurance + stay active (prolonged bed rest worsens outcome); heat or ice; physio if not improving; address yellow flags (fear of movement, catastrophising) early
2 — Paracetamol & OTC/topicalParacetamol 1 g PO q4–6h prn (max 4 g/24h); topical NSAID gel for localised pain (OTC/general advice — not an Authority supply)
3 — Oral NSAID (Authority supply)Choose by patient CV/GI/renal risk (table above); with food; shortest effective course; PPI cover if GI risk factors; not in pregnancy
4 — Adjuvants (NOT in scope)Gabapentin/pregabalin (radicular), TCAs (neuropathic), muscle relaxants — refer
5 — OpioidsNot required for mild MSK and cannot be prescribed under this Authority — refer

🗣️ Counselling (must cover)

  • Stay active — prolonged bed rest harmful for back pain
  • Heat or ice — patient preference
  • Take NSAID with food; shortest effective course
  • See a doctor if: pain relief inadequate · not improving within 5 days · symptoms worsen · new pain/symptoms
  • Avoid opioids — refer if patient requests

📋 Documentation

Electronic clinical record with explicit NSAID-choice rationale (patient CV/GI/renal risk) + pregnancy/lactation check. Yellow-flag acknowledgement. Share with GP within a week (with consent); upload to My Health Record; retain ≥7 years.

🎯 OSCE pearls

  • Age ≥18 + mild, acute mechanical stated explicitly
  • Red flag screen FIRST — cauda equina (saddle anaesthesia, bladder/bowel, bilateral sciatica) + systemic features explicit
  • Distinguish mechanical vs inflammatory pain out loud
  • Yellow flags acknowledged
  • NSAID selection by patient CV/GI/renal risk — narrate reasoning (oral list only)
  • Pregnancy → NSAID avoided; paracetamol/non-pharm; refer
  • "See a doctor if not improving in 5 days / worsening / new symptoms"
  • Document + share with GP

⚠️ Pitfalls

  • 55 yo male asking for "strong anti-inflammatory" with stroke hx + clopidogrel = bleeding risk → caution / refer
  • NSAID + ACEi + dehydration = AKI risk; avoid or short course + hydrate
  • 58 yo with hot swollen knee + fever 38.9°C + recent skin infection + diabetes = septic arthritis → ED, do NOT treat
  • Pregnant patient wanting an anti-inflammatory → NSAID not appropriate; paracetamol/non-pharm; refer
  • "Supplying analgesia and referring anyway" for systemic symptoms is wrong — systemic symptoms = refer, no supply
  • Imaging NOT indicated for acute non-specific back pain in the first weeks unless red flag
