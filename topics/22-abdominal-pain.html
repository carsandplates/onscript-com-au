🤰 Abdominal Pain — Assessment + Red Flags

Abdominal pain is NOT in NSW PATH expanded scope · Pharmacist role: recognise red flags, screen for emergencies, escalate · OTC management only for simple dyspepsia / constipation / diarrhoea per separate topics
💡 Older patients (≥50 yo) present atypically — later in course, less localised tenderness, suppressed fever/tachycardia from medications, ≥50% require admission, 20–33% require immediate surgery. 4× higher likelihood of hypothermic response to significant intra-abdominal process. Mortality 2–13%; emergency abdo surgery mortality 15–34%.

📋 Older patient atypical presentations (Lyon & Clark 2006)

Why older patients are different

  • ~25% of ED patients are >50 yo
  • Present later in course with more nonspecific symptoms
  • ≥50% require hospital admission; 20–33% require immediate surgery
  • Mortality 2–13%; emergency abdo surgery mortality 15–34%
  • 4× higher likelihood of hypothermic response to significant intra-abdominal process
  • Lower likelihood of localised tenderness despite focal surgical condition
  • Reduced rebound + guarding from decreased abdominal wall musculature
  • Suppressed tachycardia from medications or intrinsic cardiac disease
  • Variables associated with death: free air on plain film, leukocytosis with high neutrophilic band, age >84

Variables indicating need for surgery

  • Hypotension
  • Abnormal bowel sounds
  • Radiographic abnormalities (dilated loops, free air, air-fluid levels)
  • Leukocytosis

🚨 Red-flag conditions — older patient abdominal pain

Cholecystitis (most common indication for surgery >55 yo)

  • Right-sided pain, fever, vomiting
  • Murphy's sign in only ~50% of older patients
  • 60% have no back/flank pain; 5% no pain at all; 40% no nausea; >50% afebrile; 41% normal WBC; 13% have NO abnormal LFTs/fever/leukocytosis

Appendicitis

  • Older: generalised pain, longer duration, distension, rigidity, ↓ bowel sounds, mass
  • Only 23% have temp >37.7°C
  • Band count >6% + RLQ pain → 100%/90% positive predictive value
  • Correctly diagnosed only ~50% initially in older → ↑ perforation + mortality

Acute pancreatitis

  • Mortality in older 20–25% vs 8–10% general
  • Causes: alcoholism, biliary, infections, hypertriglyceridaemia, drugs, hypercalcaemia
  • Gallstone pancreatitis = 65–75% of cases
  • Mid-epigastric pain (constant), radiating to back; N/V/dehydration
  • Lipase equally sensitive, slightly more specific than amylase

Peptic ulcer disease + perforation

  • 16% of older with abdo pain have PUD
  • NSAIDs + H. pylori = strongest risk factors
  • ~30% of >60 yo with confirmed PUD have NO abdominal pain
  • Perforation: abrupt epigastric pain, absent bowel sounds, rigidity
  • 1/3 have no pain (esp. on NSAIDs)
  • Up to 50% perforated ulcers don't show free air on plain film

Diverticular disease

  • 2/3 of those >90 yo (vs 5% general)
  • >90% sigmoid → typically LLQ pain
  • 10–30% with diverticulosis develop disease/diverticulitis
  • CT modality of choice; avoid barium/colonoscopy in acute (perforation risk)

Small bowel obstruction

  • 50–70% from adhesions (previous surgery)
  • 15–30% from hernias
  • Diffuse colicky pain, N/V, altered bowel sounds, distension, dehydration, diffuse tenderness, possible mass
  • Plain film: dilated loops >2.5–3.0 cm, air-fluid levels, paucity of large bowel air
  • 25–40% radiographic findings normal
  • Gallstone ileus rare (up to 20% SBO in >65) — triad: SBO + air in biliary tree + calculus on film; mortality ≥15%

Large bowel obstruction

  • Most common cause = malignancy
  • Sigmoid volvulus = 75–80% of colonic volvulus
  • Predisposing: laxatives, sedatives, anticholinergics, anti-Parkinson meds
  • Plain film: dilated large bowel >5 cm with haustral markings
  • Perforation risk if dilation >9 cm

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA)

  • Highest risk: older males, tobacco use (90% of AAA), HTN, peripheral vascular disease, FHx
  • USPSTF: screen males 65–75 with smoking history
  • Classic triad of ruptured AAA = hypotension + back pain + pulsatile mass — present in only 25–50%
  • Often misdiagnosed as cholecystitis, perforated ulcer, diverticulitis, renal colic
  • Palpable mass + flank ecchymosis = highly suggestive of ruptured AAA
  • Ultrasound = fast bedside test for unstable patient
  • CT for stable patients or to identify leak / dissection

Acute mesenteric ischaemia

  • Uncommon but often fatal; 0.1% admissions, 1% acute abdo
  • ~50% of >45 yo have some mesenteric atherosclerosis
  • Classic: severe poorly-localised pain OUT OF PROPORTION to physical findings
  • 1/3 have N/V/D (mimics gastroenteritis)
  • Distension, shock, peritoneal signs = late (infarction/perforation)
  • Only 25% positive fecal occult blood

👻 Atypical causes — don't miss

  • UTI, pyelonephritis
  • Inferior wall MI
  • Pulmonary embolism (PE)
  • Congestive heart failure with hepatic congestion
  • Pneumonia
  • Constipation · urinary retention · abdominal muscle injury

📋 Focused assessment

  • SOCRATES the pain
  • Associated: N/V, anorexia, bowel/bladder change, weight loss, fever
  • LMP, pregnancy possibility, sexual history
  • PMH: surgery, malignancy, IBD, gallstones
  • Meds: NSAIDs, anticoagulants, opioids, SGLT2, immunosuppressants
  • Family hx (colorectal Ca, IBD)
  • Vitals: temperature, HR, BP (postural drop = volume loss)

👋 Examination sequence (Canvas — abdominal exam)

  1. Inspection (look)
  2. Auscultation (listen) — BEFORE percussion + palpation (don't alter bowel sounds)
  3. Percussion (tap)
  4. Palpation (feel) — light then deep

Preparation

  • Privacy + dignity; expose nipples-to-knees with sheet
  • Stand on patient's right
  • Patient supine, arms by side, head on pillow, pillow under knees
  • DON'T put hands above head (tightens wall)
  • Empty bladder first; warm room; relaxed patient

🎯 OSCE pearls

  • Pregnancy test in any woman of childbearing age
  • Cardiac differential for upper abdo pain (inferior MI)
  • Older patient — atypical — low threshold for referral
  • Pain out of proportion to exam = mesenteric ischaemia until proven otherwise
  • Pulsatile epigastric mass + male + smoker + ≥60 = AAA
  • Murphy's sign in cholecystitis (but 50% absent in older patients)
  • Examination sequence: inspect → auscultate → percuss → palpate

⚠️ Pitfalls

  • Missing AAA in older male smoker presenting with "back pain" + "renal colic"
  • Mesenteric ischaemia: severe pain + benign exam = paradox; don't dismiss
  • DKA presenting with abdo pain in undiagnosed T2DM
  • Ectopic pregnancy in any woman with abdo pain + LMP question
  • Suppressed signs in older patients on β-blockers, opioids, immunosuppressants
