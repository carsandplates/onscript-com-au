🤢 Nausea + Vomiting

NSW permanent service · Authority dated 5 Aug 2025 (cancelled 5 Aug 2026) · Patient ≥18 yo · Max 24h supply of a single approved medicine (ondansetron or metoclopramide) per consultation · Patients >65 yo → single dose + refer
💡 Identify the underlying cause before treating. Correct dehydration first. Most acute N/V is viral gastroenteritis or food poisoning. Short-term antiemetic use only. Use the Pregnancy-Unique Quantification of Emesis (PUQE-24) score for any pregnant patient.

✅ Eligibility

  • Patient ≥18 years
  • Acute N/V with identifiable cause and no RED flags
  • Pharmacist supply: max 24 hours of a single approved medicine (ondansetron or metoclopramide) per consultation

Gastroenterological → ED

  • Severe abdominal pain, distension, rigidity, tenderness
  • Green / bile / feculent / bloody (coffee-grounds) vomitus
  • Absolute constipation + lack of flatus (obstruction)
  • PR bleeding (incl. melaena)
  • Isolated vomiting without nausea → GP or ED

Neurological → ED

  • Severe new headache (not patient's usual)
  • Altered level of consciousness · neck stiffness / confusion / visual abnormality / seizure
  • History of head injury / trauma
  • Non-blanching rash

Metabolic → ED / GP

  • High blood sugar level (BSL) → ED
  • Low BSL without shock → GP
  • Patient on SGLT2 inhibitor → GP (euglycaemic DKA risk even with normal BSL)

Obstetric → ED

Pre-eclampsia features in pregnancy: headache, blurred vision, altered mental state, abdominal/epigastric pain, ↓ foetal movements, oliguria, peripheral oedema

Other → ED / GP

  • Chest pain / heaviness / SOB → ED
  • Hx diabetes / Addison's / immunocompromise / renal or liver failure → GP or ED
  • Recently returned from overseas → GP
  • Unclear cause of acute N/V → GP
  • Post-operative / radiation-induced / suspected drug-induced → GP or ED
  • Symptoms not resolved in 24–48h → GP
  • IM antiemetic indicated → GP

Morning sickness — first-line

  • Lifestyle: small frequent meals, dry carbs before rising
  • Ginger 250 mg QID (S2)
  • Pyridoxine (B6) 25 mg TDS ± doxylamine 12.5 mg (Restavit) — RANZCOG-endorsed first-line
  • Persistent → refer for metoclopramide / ondansetron
  • Hyperemesis gravidarum (severe + dehydration + ketonuria + >5% weight loss) → REFER ED

💊 Motion sickness (prevention)

  • Hyoscine hydrobromide: Kwells 0.3 mg / Travacalm Original 0.2 mg + caffeine + paracetamol — take 30 min pre-travel, q6h prn — anticholinergic; avoid glaucoma/BPH
  • Promethazine 25 mg 1–2h pre-travel; sedating; contraindicated <2 yo, avoid <6 yo
  • Cinnarizine (Stugeron 15 mg) 2h pre-travel; less sedating; avoid in Parkinson's
  • Acupressure wristbands (Sea-Band) — non-pharm option

💧 Rehydration (always first)

  • Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) — Hydralyte / Gastrolyte sip every 5–10 min
  • Adults: 200–400 mL after each loose stool
  • Children: 50–100 mL/kg over 4h then maintenance
  • Avoid sports drinks (wrong osmolar mismatch)
  • Unable to keep down → IV (refer ED)

🗣️ Counselling (must cover)

  • Take antiemetics 30 min before triggers (motion sickness, before meals)
  • Avoid alcohol (additive sedation)
  • Anticholinergic side effects: dry mouth, urinary retention, blurred vision, confusion in elderly
  • Driving + machinery caution for sedating agents
  • Return if no improvement at 48h, dehydration, or red flags develop

📋 Documentation

SOAP record · cause identified · drug rationale · 24h supply limit noted · safety-net advice given. Share with GP; retain ≥7 years.

🎯 OSCE pearls

  • Age ≥18 + 24h max supply stated
  • Identify underlying cause (don't just treat symptom)
  • Red flag screen — dehydration, surgical abdomen, neurological, SGLT2
  • Pregnancy + pre-eclampsia screen
  • Match mechanism → drug
  • Counsel anticholinergic SE
  • Mention ORS as first step

⚠️ Pitfalls

  • Wegovy/Ozempic (semaglutide) starter + N/V → expected; counsel + reduce dose
  • Parkinson's patient → AVOID metoclopramide (use ondansetron)
  • Patient on sotalol (QT) → ondansetron flagged "consider modification" (use metoclopramide if not Parkinson's)
  • Heart failure + hypotension + dehydration + SGLT2 → ED (DKA + AKI risk)
  • Acute dystonia from metoclopramide (tongue protrusion, oculogyric crisis) → benztropine 1–2 mg IM/IV; more common young women
  • Bilious vomiting = obstruction until proven otherwise → ED
