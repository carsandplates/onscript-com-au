🦠 Herpes Zoster (Shingles)

NSW PATH-DERM service · Skin-conditions Authority dated 29 Aug 2025 (cancelled 1 Sep 2026) · Patient ≥18 yo · Start antiviral within 72h of rash onset · Supply up to 10 days · Clinical review 48–72h
💡 Reactivation of latent varicella-zoster virus. Unilateral, dermatomal, painful vesicular rash that does NOT cross the midline. Prodrome (tingling/burning in dermatome) often precedes rash by 2–3 days. Antiviral within 72h reduces pain duration and risk of post-herpetic neuralgia, especially in patients ≥50.

✅ Eligibility

  • Adult with unilateral painful vesicular rash in a single dermatome (does not cross midline)
  • Within 72 hours of rash onset (consider beyond 72h if active vesicles, severe pain, older patient)
  • Immunocompetent (mild immunosuppression treat + refer; severe = RED)
  • No RED flags below

🔴 RED — DO NOT treat → refer

⛔ Refer without supplying antiviral — these patients need urgent specialist input or admission.

💊 Antiviral — start within 72h of rash onset

💊 Symptomatic care

  • Paracetamol 1 g PO q4–6h prn (max 4 g/24h)
  • Ibuprofen if no contraindication
  • Cool compresses, calamine lotion, non-adherent dressing on weeping lesions
  • Severe or neuropathic pain → refer GP (gabapentin / amitriptyline outside pharmacist scope)

🗣️ Counselling (must cover)

  • Take full course even if rash crusts over
  • Contagious to non-immune contacts via vesicle fluid (causes chickenpox, not zoster) — avoid pregnant women + immunocompromised contacts until lesions crust over
  • Post-herpetic neuralgia can develop after rash heals — return to GP if persistent pain
  • Once crusted, no longer infectious
  • Discuss Shingrix vaccination 6–12 months after acute episode (≥50 yo, or ≥18 if immunocompromised; 2 doses)

📋 Documentation

SOAP record in MedAdvisor: hx, exam (dermatome, midline test, Hutchinson, facial nerve), drug + rationale, safety-net. If concurrently referring (e.g. V1 involvement), include age/sex, time course, dermatome, neuropathic features, treatment commenced today (e.g. valaciclovir 1 g TDS × 7d), concern (ocular complications), specific request (urgent assessment). Share with GP; retain ≥7 years.

🎯 OSCE pearls

  • Verbalise dermatomal pattern + midline test out loud
  • Explicit Hutchinson sign check (vesicles on nose tip/side = V1 → eye involvement)
  • Facial nerve + ear canal screen (Ramsay Hunt)
  • Onset <72h check
  • Pregnancy + immunocompromise screen
  • Counsel infectiousness + isolation (causes chickenpox in non-immune)
  • Mention Shingrix prevention for future

⚠️ Pitfalls

  • Missing Hutchinson sign → blindness risk (V1 = ophthalmic division of trigeminal)
  • Forgetting renal dose adjustment
  • Adult presenting with "yellow crusts" after grandchild contact = think impetigo, not shingles (esp. if previously Shingrix-vaccinated)
  • Differential vs psoriasis: shingles is unilateral + vesicular + painful + neuropathic; psoriasis is bilateral + plaques + itch + no pain
