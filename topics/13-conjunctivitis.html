👁️ Conjunctivitis

S3 chloramphenicol pharmacist supply for uncomplicated bacterial conjunctivitis · Allergic = olopatadine / ketotifen · Refer all red-eye-with-pain or vision loss
💡 Most conjunctivitis is viral (≥80% of acute cases) and self-limiting. Bacterial = mucopurulent + sticky eyelids on waking. Allergic = bilateral + itchy + atopic history. Contact lens wearer + red eye = keratitis (Pseudomonas) until proven otherwise.

✅ Eligibility (S3 chloramphenicol)

  • Adult or child ≥2 years (per current S3 chloramphenicol protocol — verify)
  • Mucopurulent discharge; bilateral within 1–2 days; eyelids stuck on waking
  • NOT a contact lens wearer
  • NOT a neonate
  • No RED flags below

💊 Viral conjunctivitis

  • No antibiotic indicated — self-limiting 1–2 weeks; infectious 10–12 days from onset while red
  • Cool compresses · artificial tears · hand hygiene · separate towels
  • Often follows URTI; preauricular nodes tender
  • Dendritic ulcer on fluorescein = HSV → refer urgent (never steroids)

🧪 Chemical / irritant

  • IRRIGATE COPIOUSLY with saline or water ≥20 min (≥30 min if alkali; target pH 7.0–7.4 on litmus)
  • Continuous irrigation while transferring to ED
  • Lubricant drops post-irrigation
  • Refer all chemical injuries — alkali especially severe (penetrates deep)

🩸 Subconjunctival haemorrhage

  • Painless, sectoral red patch, no vision change
  • Self-limiting 1–2 weeks
  • Causes: Valsalva, cough, trauma, HTN, anticoagulant
  • Refer if recurrent, painful, vision affected, or post-trauma

💧 Eye drop technique (counsel)

  1. Hand hygiene
  2. Tilt head back, look up
  3. Pull lower lid down → pocket
  4. 1 drop in pocket (don't touch dropper to eye)
  5. Close eye; gentle pressure inner corner 1 min (↓ systemic absorption + ↑ retention)
  6. Wait ≥5 min between different drops

🗣️ Counselling (must cover)

  • Hand hygiene crucial (highly contagious)
  • Separate towels, pillowcases; no shared eye makeup; discard old makeup
  • Avoid contact lenses during infection + 24h after sx resolve
  • Replace makeup + lens case after infection
  • Children: stay home from school until 24h on antibiotics OR sx resolved (varies by school policy)

📋 Documentation

Record cause, distinguishing features used, drug + dose, drop technique counselled, safety-net. Share with GP if concerning features; retain ≥7 years.

🎯 OSCE pearls

  • Distinguish cause from history (itch + atopy = allergic; discharge + URTI = infective)
  • Vision check + photophobia + pain screen verbalised
  • Contact lens question explicit
  • Red flag systematic screen
  • Counsel drop technique
  • Hand hygiene + contagion counsel

⚠️ Pitfalls

  • Contact lens + red eye = keratitis until proven otherwise — emergency referral, NO chloramphenicol
  • Hyperacute Gonorrhoea → sight-threatening, urgent
  • Herpetic dendritic ulcer → never treat with steroids; refer
  • Acute angle-closure glaucoma → emergency (eye pain + headache + N/V + halos + cloudy cornea)
