🚫 When NOT to Prescribe

An active, evidence-based prescribing decision · Requires full clinical assessment, reasoning, and documentation · "The absence of a prescription must be justified as clearly as the presence of one"
💡 Non-prescribing protects patients from unnecessary medication harm and protects pharmacists from medico-legal risk. "The patient asked me to" is NOT a valid legal defence. Document non-prescribing with the same rigour as prescribing.

📋 Core principles

  • Most preventable harm in practice arises from unnecessary or premature treatment
  • Over-medicalisation → ↑ adverse effects, antimicrobial resistance (AMR), professional risk, medication errors
  • "The patient asked me to" is not a valid legal defence
  • Clear documentation of non-prescribing decisions strengthens defensibility
  • Prescribing outside authority criteria attracts regulatory + medico-legal risk

🛑 Triggers — when to actively consider NOT prescribing

TriggerExample
1. Early / mild disease likely self-limitingUpdated eTG for uncomplicated UTI: first-line is non-antibiotic. Number Needed to Treat (NNT) with antibiotic = 3–6 for one extra patient symptom-free at day 3. Also: tension headache, mild MSK, viral URTI, mild gastroenteritis, mild AOM (watchful waiting 48–72 h).
2. Diagnostic uncertaintyCannot confirm diagnosis to required threshold. "A red TM or pain alone is not sufficient to diagnose AOM". Atypical features → refer for assessment.
3. Outside scope criteriaAge outside service range (acne <12 yo, GORD >50 yo). Severity outside (moderate-severe acne, severe MSK). Pregnancy in services requiring referral. Exclusion criteria per each NSW Practice Standard.
4. Safety concernsAllergies, contraindications, interactions. Vulnerable populations (elderly, immunocompromised, pregnant). Combined risk factors (e.g. NSAID in elderly + CKD + ACEi + diuretic = "quadruple whammy").
5. Lack of clinical benefitTreatment not indicated by guideline. Marginal benefit ≪ side effect burden. Patient already trying optimal non-pharm.
6. Risk of harm or misuseRepeat S8 / pseudoephedrine seekers. Pattern of doctor-shopping / pharmacy-shopping. Suspected substance use disorder. Antimicrobial stewardship (AMS) — not all eligible patients need antibiotics.
7. Preventive contextUK Medical Eligibility Criteria (UKMEC) category 3–4 contraception → no supply. Risk factor profile shifts treatment from supply → refer for specialist.

📚 Case examples (Canvas)

Case 1 — Early mild uncomplicated UTI

Patient: 29 yo F, BMI 24.6, not pregnant, dysuria + frequency for 8 h. Vitals normal. No fever/flank pain/N&V/recurrent UTI/diabetes/immunocompromise/renal disease/catheter/haematuria. No exclusion criteria.

Decision: NON-PRESCRIBE — manage expectations as safety-based decision.

Plan:
  • Hydration ≥2–2.5 L/day
  • Paracetamol 1 g PO PRN (max 4 g/day) OR ibuprofen 400 mg PO q8h × up to 3 d (per eTG)
  • Avoid bladder irritants temporarily (caffeine, alcohol)
  • Safety-net: review if symptoms persist >48 h; still eligible for antibiotics (delayed prescribing); escalate urgently if T ≥38°C, flank pain, vomiting, worsening pain
Reasoning: Bayesian — probability of bacterial infection ↑ with time + progression. Time is a diagnostic discriminator. Early mild uUTI does not automatically justify antibiotic therapy.

Case 2 — Localised paediatric impetigo

Patient: 7 yo, 24 kg, no chronic illness, 2 honey-coloured crusted lesions on chin, 3 days, itchy, afebrile, no cellulitis/lymphadenopathy/mucosal involvement.

Decision: NON-PRESCRIBE oral antibiotic. Supply mupirocin 2% topical TDS × 5 days.

Approach to managing expectations:
  • Legitimise concern
  • Reframe "doing something" to "doing the best thing"
  • Explain scope limitations + legal implications
  • Neutralise precedent bias ("my doctor usually gives me...")
  • Address cost-quality misconception explicitly
Return to GP if not better at 48–72 h. Localised impetigo rarely warrants oral antibiotic therapy.

Case 3 — Combined oral contraceptive pill not eligible

Patient: 22 yo F, non-smoker, no previous hormonal contraception. Requests combined OCP. BP 148/94 (recheck after rest: 146/92), BMI 31, recurrent headaches without aura, no thromboembolic events. No regular GP.

Decision: Under UKMEC, CHC falls into category 2–3 → risk outweighs benefit. NOT eligible for CHC. Offer POP (lower risk).

Patient refuses POP: cites TikTok; refuses to accept counselling.

Plan:
  • Discuss interim barrier contraception
  • Refer to GP (outside scope, requires further assessment)
  • Leave door open for POP
  • Document UKMEC risk classification + reasoning
  • Explicit referral rationale (not "patient refuses all available options")
Preventive services + options must also consider non-prescribing processes.

🗣️ How to communicate non-prescribing (script template)

  1. Legitimise the concern: "I can see this is bothering you and I want to help."
  2. Explain the decision in plain language: "Based on what you've told me and what I can see today, the best evidence is that this will resolve without antibiotics..."
  3. Offer what you CAN do: non-pharm advice, safety-net, follow-up
  4. Reframe "doing something" as "doing the best thing"
  5. Acknowledge prior precedent without judgment: "Your GP may have prescribed this previously because [context] — today my assessment is..."
  6. Address cost/quality misconception: "More medicine isn't always better."
  7. Be explicit about safety-net: "If [specific symptom] happens, come back / go to ED."
  8. Document everything the same way you would document a prescription

⚠️ Drivers of overprescribing — recognise + counter

BiasCounter
Commission bias — fear of inactionFrame non-action as evidence-based
Patient pressure + time constraintsUse scripted reframe; don't compress consultation
Fear of missing serious pathologyRobust safety-net + follow-up plan
Anchoring bias — fixation on initial diagnosisForce reconsideration with all findings
Confirmation bias — seeking only supporting dataActively seek refuting evidence
Representativeness bias — pattern fit aloneConsider differentials beyond classic pattern
Premature closure — accepting dx before verifyingAlways include relevant negatives

🧠 Clinical reasoning process (8-step framework — Workshop 8)

  1. Presentation — symptoms
  2. Clinical history — symptoms in context
  3. Physical examination — signs
  4. Investigations — more data
  5. Problem representation — concise summary
  6. Diagnostic hypotheses — wide net first
  7. Detect or exclude red flag hypotheses
  8. Best fit of diagnosis to data
For each hypothesis, consider: epidemiology (incidence/prevalence, demographic, exposures) · time course (sudden/gradual, acute/subacute/chronic) · typical clinical presentation + variations · pathophysiological mechanism.

🛟 Safety-netting (every non-prescribing decision)

  • Specific symptoms to watch for
  • Timeframe (return if X hasn't improved in Y hours)
  • Where to go (back to pharmacy / GP / ED)
  • "If in doubt, come back"
  • Document advice given

🎯 OSCE pearls

  • Explicitly state "I'm choosing not to prescribe today because..." → list reasons
  • Frame non-prescribing as a positive evidence-based decision
  • Document AS RIGOROUSLY as prescribing
  • Safety-net every time
  • Acknowledge patient expectation
  • Offer alternative actions (non-pharm, follow-up, GP referral)
  • Use AMS language for antibiotic decisions

⚠️ Pitfalls

  • "Just give them something" mindset → AMR + adverse events
  • Not documenting non-prescribing reasoning → indefensible if outcome bad
  • Forgetting safety-net → patient deteriorates without escalation pathway
  • Letting patient pressure override clinical judgement
  • Missing diagnosis by anchoring on initial impression (e.g. "looks like impetigo" without considering shingles in elderly with Shingrix hx)
