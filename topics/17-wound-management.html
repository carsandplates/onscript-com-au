🩹 Minor Wound Management

NSW planned/pilot service · Minor superficial/partial-thickness wounds (abrasion, minor laceration, skin tear, minor burn) · Mandatory tetanus assessment for all traumatic wounds
💡 Most minor wounds do NOT require antibiotics. Always assess: pain control, infection risk, tetanus prophylaxis status. Moist wound healing (Winter 1960s principle) heals faster than dry — emollients/dressings retain moisture.

✅ Eligibility (pharmacist scope — minor wounds)

  • Adult or paediatric with superficial / partial-thickness acute wound (abrasion, minor laceration, skin tear Category 1, minor burn)
  • Patient can manage at home; access to follow-up
  • No RED flags below

💊 Management — minor wound steps

  1. Cleanse: normal saline preferred (tap water OK in most minor wounds)
  2. Haemostasis: direct pressure 10–15 min; elevate
  3. Closure (if needed): Steri-Strips for clean superficial linear lacerations; tissue adhesive (Dermabond) for non-tension wounds <5 cm
  4. Dressing (see selection table below)
  5. Tetanus status assessment (see below)

CategoryDescriptionAction
Category 1No skin loss (linear OR flap-type with flap that can be repositioned to cover wound bed)Cleanse · roll flap back · silicone dressing · mark arrows for removal direction · leave 5 days for "take"
Category 2Partial flap loss⚠️ Refer plastics within 24–48 h (skin graft viability)
Category 3Total flap loss (full thickness exposed)⚠️ Refer plastics within 24–48 h

💊 Pain management

  • Assume all wounds painful until patient says otherwise
  • Numerical 0–10 + verbal descriptor (none/mild/mod/severe)
  • Reassess regularly; pain over time = clue to infection / poor healing
  • Nociceptive (gnawing, aching, tender) — paracetamol, NSAID, codeine, opioids
  • Neuropathic (burning, stinging, shooting) — amitriptyline, gabapentin, pregabalin (refer)
  • Non-pharm: distraction, music, rest, positioning, supports
  • >40% of leg ulcers have neuropathic symptoms

🗣️ Counselling (must cover)

  • Dressing change schedule + technique
  • Signs of infection — return immediately (red, warm, swollen, painful, exudate, fever)
  • Tetanus status checked + addressed
  • Keep wound clean + dry between dressing changes
  • Pain management plan

📋 Documentation

Wound assessment (TIME), classification (ISTAP if skin tear), tetanus status, treatment supplied (dressing + antibiotic if relevant), advice given, follow-up plan. Photo if possible. Share with GP for ongoing wounds; retain ≥7 years.

🎯 OSCE pearls

  • Immediate escalation criteria stated explicitly
  • Wound assessment using TIME framework
  • Dressing matched to wound bed + exudate + depth
  • Tetanus status check — mandatory for every traumatic wound
  • Skin tear category determines plastics referral urgency (Cat 2/3 = 24–48h)
  • Counsel on infection signs (red, warm, swollen, painful, exudate, fever)

⚠️ Pitfalls

  • ISTAP Category 2/3 needs 24–48 h plastics review for graft viability — don't sit on it
  • "Fight bite" (human bite over knuckle) = high infection risk — antibiotic + tetanus + refer
  • Negative pressure / suction devices = specialist
  • Necrotic black eschar may need debridement — refer
