🧣 Focused Exam: Neck, Thyroid & MSK ROM

OSCE Stations 2 & 3 (focused physical assessment) · Fills the exam-pool gaps not covered elsewhere — thyroid/neck + neck and lower-back range of motion · Derm, abdomen, CV/resp, vitals + ear/nose have their own pages
💡 Every focused exam: wash hands · introduce · consent · expose adequately · chaperone · compare left vs right · narrate findings.

🧣 Thyroid / neck examination

Inspect (front)

  • Goitre / lumps / swelling, scars, asymmetry, pulsations
  • Swallow test: give water — thyroid and thyroglossal masses move up on swallowing
  • Tongue protrusion: a thyroglossal cyst rises with tongue protrusion (midline)

Palpate (from behind)

  • Palpate both lobes + isthmus; ask patient to swallow again
  • Note size, symmetry, nodules (single vs multinodular), tenderness, mobility, consistency
  • Trachea — confirm midline (deviation = mass effect → refer)
  • Carotid pulses: palpate one side at a time — NEVER both at once; auscultate for a bruit → partial obstruction → refer

Cervical lymph nodes (systematic)

Submental → submandibular → pre-/post-auricular → occipital → anterior cervical → posterior cervical → supraclavicular (Virchow's node — sinister). Tender = reactive/infective; hard, fixed, non-tender = sinister → refer.

Thyroid-status clues

HyperthyroidHypothyroid
Fine tremor, warm sweaty palms, tachycardia/AF, lid lag, exophthalmos, weight lossBradycardia, dry/coarse skin, slow-relaxing reflexes, weight gain, cold intolerance

🔄 Neck MSK range of motion

  • Movements: flexion (chin to chest), extension, lateral flexion (ear to shoulder) L/R, rotation (chin to shoulder) L/R
  • Compare sides; note pain, limitation, crepitus
🔴 RED FLAG — recent trauma / ?C-spine injury → DO NOT move the neck, immobilise + ED. Also refer for neuro deficit, severe pain, or fever + neck stiffness (?meningism).

🔄 Lower back MSK range of motion

  • Inspect: posture, scoliosis, muscle wasting, scars
  • Movements: flexion (reach toward toes), extension, lateral flexion L/R, rotation
  • Straight leg raise (radicular/sciatic pain reproduction)
  • Palpate spinous processes + paraspinal muscles for tenderness/spasm
🔴 RED FLAGS → ED: cauda equina (saddle anaesthesia, bladder/bowel dysfunction, bilateral leg symptoms) · significant trauma · fever + back pain (?infection) · unexplained weight loss + night pain (?malignancy).

🎯 OSCE pearls (Stations 2 & 3)

  • Wash hands, introduce, consent, expose adequately, chaperone — every exam
  • Thyroid: palpate from behind + use the swallow test; state the trachea is midline
  • Never palpate both carotids simultaneously; auscultate for a bruit
  • Examine lymph nodes systematically, naming each chain
  • ROM: demonstrate then ask patient to copy; compare left vs right; narrate findings
  • State the relevant red flags that would stop the exam and trigger referral
