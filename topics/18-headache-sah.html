🤕 Headache Red Flags + Ottawa SAH Rule

Pharmacist role: recognise red flags, escalate to ED, document · OTC analgesia only for primary headache after ruling out red flags · Subarachnoid Haemorrhage (SAH) = bleeding in space around brain
💡 The Ottawa Subarachnoid Haemorrhage (SAH) Rule is a rule-out tool for ED: 100% sensitive, 15% specific. SAH = ~1% of ED headache presentations. Pharmacist applies the principle — any positive criterion + meets inclusion = refer ED for non-contrast head CT.

🔴 RED — ED IMMEDIATELY (SNNOOP10 + Ottawa SAH triggers)

⛔ Do NOT supply analgesia and discharge. These need urgent imaging.

Other emergencies

  • Neck pain / stiffness (meningitis, SAH)
  • Witnessed loss of consciousness
  • Onset during exertion
  • Limited neck flexion on examination
  • Recent head injury / trauma
  • Non-blanching rash
  • Headache + altered mental state / confusion
  • Headache + vomiting + visual disturbance (raised ICP)
  • "Worst headache of my life"

🚨 Ottawa SAH Rule — when to apply + criteria

Inclusion criteria (rule applies if ALL met)

Criterion
Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score = 15/15 (alert and oriented)
No fall or head trauma in past 7 days
Presentation within 14 days of headache onset
Patient aged ≥15 years
Headache is new, severe, nontraumatic, reaching maximum intensity within 1 hour

Exclusion criteria (do NOT use the rule)

  • Previously established history of headache syndrome
  • Referral from another institution with confirmed SAH
  • Returning for reassessment of headache previously CT + lumbar puncture
  • Papilloedema present
  • New focal neurological deficits
  • Previous diagnosis of cerebral aneurysm, SAH, brain tumour, hydrocephalus
  • Chronic recurrent headaches (≥3 same character/intensity over ≥6 months)

Rule = workup for SAH if ANY positive

Criterion
Age ≥40
Neck pain or stiffness
Witnessed loss of consciousness
Onset during exertion
Thunderclap headache (instantly peaking pain)
Limited neck flexion on examination
Statistical performance: 100% sensitive (rule-out tool), 15% specific. Bellolio 2015 external validation: sensitivity 100%, specificity 7.6%.

🟠 ORANGE — same-day GP / clinic

  • New persistent headache without other red flags
  • New chronic daily headache
  • Change in pattern of long-standing headache
  • Headache with mild visual symptoms (no acute loss)
  • Worsening despite optimal OTC analgesia
  • Suspected medication-overuse headache (>10 days/month opioid/triptan; >15 days/month simple analgesia)
  • Recurrent debilitating migraines without diagnosis

✅ Eligibility for OTC pharmacist analgesia

  • Primary headache (migraine, tension-type, cluster) — known diagnosis OR clearly typical for patient
  • No red flag criteria
  • Headache pattern matches patient's previous attacks
  • No medication overuse pattern

🧠 Primary headache types (recognise)

TypeFeatures
Tension-type (most common)Bilateral, pressing/tightening, mild-moderate, no aggravation by activity. No N/V; photo/phonophobia only mild.
MigraineUnilateral often, throbbing, mod-severe, aggravated by activity. N/V + photo/phonophobia. ± aura (visual scotoma, sensory, speech) usually <60 min before headache.
Cluster ("suicide headache")Unilateral, periorbital, excruciating. Ipsilateral autonomic features: lacrimation, rhinorrhoea, ptosis, miosis, conjunctival injection, eyelid oedema. Attacks 15 min–3h, occur daily-weekly in clusters. Treatment: high-flow O₂ + sumatriptan SC → refer.

📋 Focused headache assessment (SOCRATES + extras)

  • SOCRATES (Site, Onset, Character, Radiation, Associations, Time, Exacerbating/Relieving, Severity)
  • Time to peak (thunderclap = within 1 minute)
  • Triggers (food, alcohol, stress, exertion, sex, postural)
  • Associated: N/V, photophobia, phonophobia, aura, neurological symptoms
  • PMH: previous headaches, cancer, pregnancy, anticoagulant
  • Medications: analgesic use frequency (medication-overuse risk)
  • Functional impact + sleep
  • Recent trauma, fever, weight loss

🗣️ Counselling (must cover)

  • Headache diary (frequency, severity, triggers, meds)
  • Avoid known triggers
  • Hydration, sleep hygiene, stress management
  • Limit analgesia to <10 days/month (opioid/triptan) or <15 days/month (simple) to avoid medication-overuse headache
  • Return / present to ED for any red flag development

🎯 OSCE pearls

  • Apply red-flag screen first — verbalise SNNOOP10 or list
  • Onset speed (thunderclap?) explicit
  • "Worst-ever" question
  • Neck stiffness assessment
  • Pregnancy + medication history
  • Counsel medication-overuse risk
  • Don't supply triptans without confirming previous safe use + no contraindications

⚠️ Pitfalls

  • "Worst headache of my life" = SAH until proven otherwise → ED
  • Sudden onset >50 yo + new = ED (high SAH suspicion)
  • Headache + fever + neck stiffness = meningitis → ED
  • Headache + jaw claudication + visual symptoms + age >50 = GCA → urgent (steroids within hours to save vision)
  • Pregnant + severe headache + visual symptoms + epigastric pain = pre-eclampsia → ED
