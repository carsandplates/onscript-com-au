💊 Contraception Initiation

New-start / method-selection consult · OSCE Station 1 (history + cultural safety) and Station 4 (diagnosis + management) · For the continuation/resupply service see the separate Oral Contraception (Resupply) page
💡 Initiation = prescribing competency. NSW's pharmacist class authority covers continuation only — initiating a new method is part of the qualified pharmacist-prescriber role. Method selection follows the UK Medical Eligibility Criteria (UKMEC) + eTG + RANZCOG. Always exclude pregnancy before quick-starting.

✅ The initiation consult — history

Structure straight from the Canvas contraception workshop.
  • Medical history — VTE, migraine ± aura, BP, liver disease, breast cancer, diabetes, IHD/stroke
  • Current and/or previous contraception — what's been used, tolerability, reason for switching
  • Reproductive & menstrual history — LMP, cycle, parity, pregnancy plans, breastfeeding/postpartum
  • Lifestyle factors — smoking, BMI, alcohol
  • Current medications + interactions — enzyme inducers, lamotrigine
  • Explore patient preference — daily vs LARC, bleeding pattern, reversibility, needle aversion
  • Identify red flags needing referral (below); exclude pregnancy

⏩ Quick-starting (FSRH) — additional precautions

Start any day if reasonably certain not pregnant; otherwise quick-start + pregnancy test in 3–4 weeks.

Cu-IUD0 days — effective immediately
POP (traditional + desogestrel / drospirenone)2 days

DMPA (Depo)7 days

After levonorgestrel EC → start the hormonal method immediately (+ precautions above). After ulipristal (UPA) EC → wait 120 h (5 days) before starting a hormonal method (UPA + progestogen interact).

🦠 STIs & contraception

  • Most contraceptives do NOT protect against STIs — only condoms do → promote dual protection
  • Assess STI risk before initiating (new/multiple partners, inconsistent condoms, STI Hx); screen/refer if indicated
  • Red flags (discharge, pelvic pain, dyspareunia, abnormal bleeding) → investigate before initiating

🪶 Cultural safety & coercive control

  • Private, non-judgemental space; ask who is in the room; consider seeing the patient alone
  • Indicators of coercion/abuse: partner answering for patient, contraception hidden/sabotaged, repeated EC requests, unexplained injuries, fear/reluctance
  • Legal/ethical: consent, confidentiality, mandatory reporting where a child is at risk
  • Referral: 1800RESPECT (1800 737 732) · Full Stop Australia · 13YARN (13 92 76) · NSW Health Sexual Assault Services

🤰 Postpartum (Ellen case)

  • <6 weeks postpartum + breastfeeding: CHC contraindicated (UKMEC 4) → POP, implant, DMPA, or IUD (insert <48 h or ≥4 wk)
  • Lactational amenorrhoea reliable only if <6 months, fully breastfeeding, amenorrhoeic
  • Discuss timing, breastfeeding compatibility, future pregnancy spacing

🔁 Follow-up / switching

  • Follow up: CHC/POP — review BP + tolerability ~3 months after start, then annually; DMPA — 12-weekly; implant/IUD — once first bleed settles
  • Side effects: most settle by 3 months (counsel persistence); review if persistent/intolerable
  • Switching: bridge methods to avoid a contraceptive gap (continue old method or use condoms during the additional-precautions window)

🎯 OSCE pearls (initiation)

  • Take a full contraceptive + reproductive history and explore preference — don't just hand over a pill
  • Measure + state BP, HR, BMI aloud; do the thyroid/neck + DERM (hirsutism) + CV calf checks where indicated
  • Verbalise UKMEC for the chosen method (especially CHC 4 conditions)
  • Exclude pregnancy and state the quick-start precaution window for the method chosen
  • Screen for coercive control; name the referral pathway if disclosed
  • Offer LARC and dual protection; arrange follow-up + safety-net
