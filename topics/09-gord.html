🔥 GORD / Dyspepsia

NSW permanent service · Patient ≥18 AND ≤50 years · Up to 4 weeks supply · NSW Approved Medicines dated 25 February 2026
💡 GORD = frequent (≥2 episodes/week) heartburn or regurgitation severe enough to impair quality of life, OR with complications. >50 yo with new symptoms = REFER. Lifestyle first → antacid/alginate → H2RA → PPI.
⚠️ No "treat-and-refer" pathway exists for this service. If the patient meets any red flag or referral criterion, they are NOT eligible under the Authority → refer (no supply). The two tiers below differ only in urgency of referral (ED vs GP) — not in whether you treat.

✅ Eligibility

  • Patient ≥18 AND ≤50 years
  • Cardinal symptoms: heartburn + regurgitation (atypical: belching, epigastric pain, dyspepsia, acid brash)
  • No red flags or referral criteria below; consent obtained; patient present
Extra-oesophageal symptoms (enamel erosion, halitosis, hoarseness/throat clearing, persistent cough, sore throat, sinusitis, chest pain, sleep disturbance, nausea, asthma-like symptoms) are associated with but not specific to reflux → refer for investigation.

🔴 RED FLAG warning signs → immediate referral to a medical practitioner (ED if cardiac/bleeding)

Verbatim from the Practice Standard. Any one = refer, no supply.

Assessment of chest & abdominal pain is complex — GORD and AMI can mimic each other and co-exist. Maintain a high index of suspicion; suspected AMI → immediate ED.

🟠 Other referral criteria → not eligible, refer to GP (no supply)

Verbatim from the Practice Standard. These are exclusion criteria — meeting any means refer, no pharmacist supply (not "treat and refer").
  • Patient has any red flag warning sign (above)
  • A clear diagnosis of gastro-oesophageal reflux or GORD cannot be made
  • Patient is aged under 18 or over 50 years
  • Patient is being managed by a gastroenterologist
  • The condition is having a marked negative emotional or social effect on the patient
  • Inadequate response / symptom control inadequate after 4 weeks of PPI at the standard dose
  • Symptoms worsen
  • Atypical symptoms or signs
  • Patient requires long-term PPI therapy and has not been investigated or reviewed by a medical practitioner
  • Patients with a stoma

📎 Supplementary aid (eTG / background — NOT a Practice Standard criterion)

ALARMS (alarm-symptom mnemonic): Anaemia · Loss of weight · Anorexia · Recent onset progressive · Melaena/haematemesis · Swallowing difficulty.
Memory aid only — the Standard's red-flag list is broader (also chest pain, MI signs, vomiting, jaundice, diaphoresis, abdominal pain, severe/frequent/changing symptoms, any new/changed symptoms over 50).

🩺 Medicines to review at assessment (eTG / AMH — not a referral flag)

May worsen reflux: CCBs, nitrates, theophylline, anticholinergics, bisphosphonates, NSAIDs, oestrogen, caffeine.
Can cause oesophagitis directly: bisphosphonates, tetracyclines, iron, potassium, NSAIDs.

💊 Stepwise management

StepAction
1 — Lifestyle (always)Weight loss if BMI ↑ · elevate head of bed (blocks not pillows) · avoid lying within ~3h of meals · smaller meals · avoid triggers (alcohol, caffeine, fatty/spicy, chocolate, peppermint, citrus) · smoking/vaping cessation · avoid tight clothing
2 — Antacids / alginates PRN (mild intermittent)Calcium carbonate (Quick-Eze) · Al/Mg hydroxide (Mylanta — Al constipates, Mg causes diarrhoea) · Alginate (Gaviscon) — raft on stomach contents · separate from other meds by ~2h (chelation: tetracyclines, FQ, levothyroxine, iron, bisphosphonates)
3 — H2RAFamotidine 20 mg PO BD; onset 30–60 min; good for nocturnal symptoms; tachyphylaxis at 2–6 wk
4 — PPI (first-line for typical GORD)Take 30 min before breakfast. Trial can confirm diagnosis. Step-down once controlled to lowest effective dose / PRN. Review at 4 weeks — inadequate response at standard dose → refer.

📋 Long-term PPI counselling

  • Step down or use PRN preferred; stop if no ongoing indication; review regularly
  • Risks (modest individually): B12, magnesium, iron deficiency; fracture risk (DEXA if risk factors); C. difficile; slight ↑ CKD risk
  • Rebound hypersecretion on abrupt cessation — taper
  • Long-term therapy in a patient not previously reviewed by a medical practitioner → refer

👶 Pregnancy

  • Antacids + H2RA generally used at standard doses
  • PPIs have different levels of evidence — be guided by current safety/efficacy data; most PPIs TGA Category B3 · rabeprazole B1
  • If a PPI is required: omeprazole has the most clinical experience
  • Resource: MotherSafe — Heartburn in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

🗣️ Counselling (must cover)

  • Lifestyle first — esp. weight, head-of-bed, no late meals
  • PPI 30 min before breakfast — narrate this (students lose marks for "with food")
  • Antacid spacing ~2h from other meds
  • Review at 4 weeks; step down or PRN once controlled
  • Return if any red-flag / ALARMS feature develops

📋 Documentation

Electronic clinical record per NSW Authority. PPI–clopidogrel check documented if relevant. Step-down plan. Share with GP within a week (with consent); upload to My Health Record; retain ≥7 years.

🎯 OSCE pearls

  • Age ≥18 AND ≤50 stated explicitly (>50 = refer)
  • Red-flag / alarm screen verbalised (ALARMS as memory aid, but cover the full list)
  • Cardiac differential acknowledged (chest pain → ED)
  • Medication review (causative drugs)
  • Lifestyle counsel first
  • PPI dosing: 30 min before breakfast
  • Step-down plan + 4-week review; up to 4-week supply maximum

⚠️ Pitfalls

  • 54 yo with chest burning + nausea + SOB + smoking + HTN + T2DM → ED (rule out MI); also >50 = ineligible
  • 45 yo on clopidogrel → rabeprazole (NOT omeprazole/esomeprazole — interaction)
  • "Atypical symptoms" or "marked emotional/social effect" → refer, no supply (exclusion criteria, not treat-and-refer)
  • Pregnancy GORD: lifestyle, alginate, calcium carbonate first; omeprazole if PPI needed
  • Refer all new GORD >50 yo — could be malignancy
