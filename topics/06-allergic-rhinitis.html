👃 Allergic + Non-Allergic Rhinitis

NSW permanent service · Patient ≥6 years · Pharmacist may supply up to 4 weeks therapy before GP referral · NSW Approved Medicines list dated 25 February 2026
💡 Stepwise approach by severity (Allergic Rhinitis and its Impact on Asthma — ARIA). Intranasal Corticosteroid (INCS) is the most effective treatment for moderate–severe rhinitis, especially nasal obstruction. Takes 4 weeks for full effect — counsel patience.

✅ Eligibility

  • Patient ≥6 years
  • Allergic OR non-allergic rhinitis (NOT infectious rhinosinusitis)
  • No RED flags below

💊 Stepwise treatment (NSW flowchart)

StepSymptomsTreatment
1Always — non-pharmacologicalTrigger avoidance · saline nasal irrigation (strong evidence) · shower at night during pollen season
2MildOral OR intranasal antihistamine (loratadine 10 mg, cetirizine 10 mg, fexofenadine 120 mg daily for AR, desloratadine 5 mg)
3Moderate–severeOral/intranasal antihistamine PLUS intranasal corticosteroid (INCS) — max effect at 2 weeks
4Persistent obstructionCombination intranasal antihistamine + corticosteroid (olopatadine + mometasone)
5Ocular symptoms persistentAdd olopatadine 0.1% eye drops
6Intermittent AR + co-existing asthmaConsider montelukast — ⚠️ FDA black-box: mood / behaviour / suicidality — counsel + monitor
7Persistent despite optimal RxRefer for allergen immunotherapy
⛔ Non-allergic rhinitis: antihistamines NOT indicated. INCS is the mainstay.
Combining oral + intranasal antihistamine = no additional benefit.
Oral + intranasal decongestants have NO role in allergic rhinitis per NSW protocol.

💊 INCS technique — high-yield

  • Aim spray AWAY from septum (toward outer wall of nostril) → reduces epistaxis + septal damage
  • Prime spray before first use + after long gap
  • Use even when asymptomatic during high-trigger period
  • Onset: days; max effect at 2 weeks — counsel patience

👁️ Allergic conjunctivitis (eye drops)

  • Olopatadine 0.1% drops BD (NSW approved)
  • Alternative: ketotifen 0.025%, cromoglycate, lodoxamide (mast cell stabilisers); azelastine
  • NOT within scope: anti-inflammatory eye drops (ketorolac, corticosteroids)
  • NOT recommended: vasoconstrictor eye drops (naphazoline, tetryzoline)

⚠️ Decongestants — caution

  • Topical (oxymetazoline, xylometazoline): max 5–7 days (rhinitis medicamentosa = rebound)
  • Systemic (pseudoephedrine): S3, contraindicated HTN / IHD / glaucoma / BPH / hyperthyroidism
  • Per NSW protocol, decongestants have NO role in allergic rhinitis

🗣️ Counselling (must cover)

  • Saline nasal irrigation first-line (cheap, effective)
  • INCS technique (aim away from septum)
  • Set expectation: max effect at 2 weeks — don't stop early
  • Screen for asthma co-existence (AR + asthma in ~80%)
  • Warn about decongestant rebound

📋 Documentation + review

Mild + adequately managed on antihistamine → no formal follow-up. INCS started → review after 4 weeks. No improvement after 4 weeks optimal therapy → refer GP. Share with GP; retain ≥7 years.

🎯 OSCE pearls

  • Age ≥6 yo + 4-week supply window stated explicitly
  • Distinguish allergic vs infective vs non-allergic from history
  • Classify by ARIA (intermittent/persistent + mild/mod-severe)
  • INCS technique narrated (aim away from septum, prime spray)
  • Set expectation: max effect at 2 weeks
  • Saline irrigation mentioned explicitly
  • Screen for asthma co-existence
  • Warn re decongestant rebound

💎 Pearls

  • "Allergic salute" in child → transverse nasal crease (chronic nose-rubbing upward)
  • Allergic shiners = dark periorbital circles from venous congestion
  • AR + asthma coexist in ~80% of asthmatics
  • INCS more effective than oral antihistamine for moderate-severe
