🧠 Mental Health Red Flags

Pharmacist role: recognise + escalate · NOT a treatment scope · Often first-line contact
💡 Asking about suicide does NOT increase risk — it reduces it. Use direct language. Direct questioning normalises disclosure. The Pharmacist's job is to recognise, communicate without judgement, and escalate appropriately. Mental Health First Aid course is highly recommended (PSA-endorsed).

🔴 IMMINENT RISK → 000 / accompany to ED

⛔ Do NOT leave alone. Involve next of kin if able. Make the call yourself — don't leave it to the patient.
  • Active suicidal intent + clear plan + means
  • Recent attempt
  • Command hallucinations
  • Severe agitation
  • Acute psychosis with risk to self or others
  • Patient unable to keep self safe
  • Recent overdose / attempt

Other presentations needing same-day escalation

  • Self-harm (cutting, burning) — often coping mechanism, not suicidal intent, but ↑ risk; ask about frequency, methods, escalation
  • Acute psychosis — hallucinations, delusions, disorganised thought/speech, behavioural change, lack of insight
  • Mania / hypomania — ↓ sleep need, pressured speech, flight of ideas, grandiosity, risky behaviour, spending sprees
  • Severe depression — pervasive low mood ≥2 weeks + anhedonia + sleep/appetite change + worthlessness + functional impairment
  • Anxiety crisis / panic — sudden intense fear, chest pain, palpitations, SOB, sweating, derealisation. Rule out cardiac first if first presentation.

🗣️ How to ask about suicide (the right way)

Use plain, direct language. Direct questioning does NOT plant the idea.
  • "Are you having any thoughts of harming yourself?"
  • "Are you thinking about suicide?"
  • "Have you thought about how you might do it?" (assesses planning)
  • "Do you have access to [means]?"
  • "What's stopping you?" (protective factors — family, religion, future plans)
Avoid: "You're not going to do anything stupid, are you?" — closes the conversation.

🏥 In-pharmacy approach

  1. Move to private area (consultation room)
  2. Sit, not stand — non-threatening, eye-level
  3. Listen without judgement — open questions, reflect, don't problem-solve
  4. Validate — "That sounds incredibly hard"
  5. Ask directly about suicide if indicated
  6. Assess plan, means, protective factors
  7. Don't promise confidentiality you can't keep — duty of care if risk
  8. Co-create safety plan if low/moderate risk
  9. Make the call to GP / MH team / 000 — don't leave to patient
  10. Document thoroughly; brief manager if appropriate

🎯 OSCE pearls

  • Private setting
  • Non-judgemental tone
  • Direct questioning about suicide if indicated
  • Risk stratification verbalised
  • Safety plan: means restriction, support contacts, follow-up
  • Provide specific Australian resource numbers (Lifeline 13 11 14)
  • Documentation + GP communication
  • Don't leave alone if high risk
  • Self-care acknowledgement (this is hard work)

💎 Pearls

  • Asking about suicide does NOT plant the idea — strong evidence, repeated meta-analyses
  • Postpartum women, men 25–44 yo, Aboriginal + Torres Strait Islander populations, LGBTQ+ youth, rural farmers = elevated risk groups
  • Antidepressants take 2–4 weeks for mood lift; energy/activation improves first → paradoxical risk window
  • Lithium therapeutic 0.6–1.0 mmol/L; toxic >1.5
