🍄 Skin — Fungal Infections + Eczema

Atopic dermatitis = NSW PATH-DERM permanent service (mild-mod) · Fungal infections = OTC + S2 antifungals · Candidiasis = topical/oral
💡 Atopic dermatitis flares are in NSW PATH-DERM scope; tinea and candidiasis are OTC. Eczema herpeticum (HSV superinfection of eczema) = EMERGENCY — monomorphic punched-out vesicles + fever + unwell patient.

🟠 ORANGE — refer (topical insufficient)

  • Tinea capitis → oral antifungal (refer)
  • Tinea unguium (nails) — topical <20% success → oral terbinafine 12 wk fingernails / 24 wk toenails (refer)
  • Extensive disease / immunocompromised → oral terbinafine 250 mg daily 2–6 weeks (refer)
  • Failed topical after 4 weeks
  • Diabetes / circulation issues with foot infection
  • Suspected secondary bacterial infection

💊 Treatment (per eTG)

🗣️ Counselling

  • Keep area dry (drying matters more than antifungal)
  • Loose cotton clothing, change daily; don't share towels/socks
  • Treat athlete's foot to prevent recurrent tinea corporis (autoinoculation)
  • Thoroughly dry between toes after shower

🟢 Vulvovaginal candidiasis (thrush)

✅ Eligibility (OTC supply)

  • Adult, not pregnant, not breastfeeding
  • Classic symptoms: vulval itch, thick white "cottage cheese" discharge, no offensive odour
  • NOT first episode (uncertain diagnosis — refer to GP for swab)
  • No RED flags below

🔴 RED — DO NOT treat → refer GP

Trigger

Pregnancy (oral azoles contraindicated; topical OK)

Bleeding · postmenopausal

👶 Oral candidiasis (thrush)

  • Infants: miconazole 2% oral gel QID after feeds × 7 days
  • Adults: nystatin oral suspension 100,000 U/mL — 1 mL QID × 7 d; OR miconazole 2% oral gel QID
  • Investigate cause: inhaled steroid (rinse mouth), denture hygiene, immunocompromise

🟢 Atopic Eczema / Dermatitis (NSW PATH-DERM)

Recognise

  • Itch (cardinal symptom) + erythema + scaling + lichenification
  • Distribution: infants → face, scalp, extensor; older → flexural (antecubital, popliteal); adults → hands, neck, eyelids
  • Personal / family hx atopy (asthma, allergic rhinitis)
  • Triggers: soap, stress, infection, dust mite, heat, food (less common)

✅ Eligibility (NSW PATH-DERM)

  • Mild-moderate atopic dermatitis flare
  • No mucosal/eye involvement
  • No RED flags below

🔴 RED — DO NOT treat → refer urgently

💊 Stepwise management

Step 1 — Always: skincare foundation

  • Emollient frequency >> potency — apply 2–4× daily, immediately after bathing while skin damp
  • Soap-free wash (Cetaphil, QV, DermaVeen)
  • Lukewarm short showers (<10 min)
  • Cotton clothing; avoid wool, synthetics
  • Aqueous cream NOT recommended as leave-on (SLS irritant) — wash only

Step 2 — Topical corticosteroid for flares (match potency to body site)

PotencyDrugBody sites
Mild ✅Hydrocortisone 1%Face, eyelids, axillae, groin, infants
ModerateMethylprednisolone aceponate 0.1% (Advantan), triamcinolone 0.02% (Aristocort)Trunk, limbs
PotentBetamethasone valerate 0.1% (Betnovate), mometasone 0.1% (Elocon)Thick skin: palms, soles, scalp, lichenified
Very potentClobetasol 0.05% (Clobex)Specialist only, short course

Fingertip Unit (FTU) counsel

1 FTU = distal crease to fingertip = covers 2 adult palms. Adult body area estimates: face + neck 2.5 FTU · trunk front 7 FTU · trunk back 7 FTU · 1 arm 3 FTU · 1 hand 1 FTU · 1 leg 6 FTU · 1 foot 2 FTU.

Apply BD to active areas; stop when clear (usually 7–14 days); step down to maintenance.

Step 3 — Calcineurin inhibitors (steroid-sparing — refer for prescription)

  • Tacrolimus 0.03–0.1% ointment OR pimecrolimus 1% cream — for face/eyelids/sensitive areas
  • BD until clear, then maintenance 2–3×/week
  • Burning sensation common initially (resolves); avoid sun (use SPF)

Step 4 — Systemic (refer)

  • Dupilumab, methotrexate, ciclosporin — specialist

🗣️ Counselling (must cover)

  • Trigger avoidance + emollient adherence (used 2× more than steroid)
  • Match steroid potency to body site
  • FTU counsel (1 FTU = 2 adult palms)
  • Time-limit + step-down plan
  • Bath + soap habits
  • Watch for infection signs — return immediately
  • Eczema herpeticum education (punched-out vesicles + fever = emergency)

🎯 OSCE pearls

  • Confirm trigger avoidance + emollient adherence
  • Match steroid potency to body site
  • FTU counsel
  • Time-limit + step-down plan
  • Bath + soap habits
  • Refer eczema herpeticum (high-stakes miss)

💎 Pearls

  • Emollients are primary therapy — use 2× more than steroid
  • Wet wraps for severe paediatric — refer for instruction
  • Steroid phobia common — counsel safe use; atrophy reversible if mild + intermittent
  • Bleach baths (¼ cup bleach in full bath, 2×/week) reduce S. aureus colonisation in moderate-severe
  • Eczema herpeticum — punched-out vesicles + unwell → EMERGENCY
