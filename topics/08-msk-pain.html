🦴 Acute Musculoskeletal (MSK) Pain

NSW permanent service · Patient ≥18 yo · Mild mechanical pain only · NSW Approved Medicines list dated 25 February 2026
💡 NSAID selection is guided by safety profile (GI, CV, renal risk), not by analgesic potency differences. Use the shortest effective course. Always combine with non-pharmacological measures. Always plan a taper / stop point at start.

✅ Eligibility

  • Patient ≥18 years (over 50 yo = ↑ caution for serious underlying pathology)
  • Mechanical MSK pain (damage to tissue/joints/vertebrae/muscle from movement, fall, sprain)
  • Mild intensity (mod–severe = refer)
  • Self-limiting expected (<3 months)
  • No RED flags below

🔴 RED — DO NOT treat → refer urgently

TUNAFISH back-pain mnemonic

Trauma · Unexplained weight loss · Neurological deficit · Age <20 or >50 new · Fever / IVDU / immunocompromised · Intractable night pain · Steroid long-term, osteoporosis · Hx of cancer

Patient categories — refer GP (RED)

  • Patient <18 years
  • Pain not attributable to acute mechanical MSK (chronic / inflammatory)
  • Hx complex rheumatological condition (lupus, RA, psoriatic arthritis, dermatomyositis, scleroderma) — even acute flare
  • Mod–severe pain or significant functional limitation
  • Intoxicated · unable to rate pain (cognitive impairment)
  • Patient requests opioid OR opioid-tolerant / recovering from opioid use disorder
  • High risk of Acute Rheumatic Fever (ARF), age <40 yo: ATSI / Māori / Pacific in disadvantaged household · hx ARF/RHD · household member with recent ARF/RHD · overcrowding + low socioeconomic status · current/frequent travel to high-ARF settings

⚠️ NSAID GI / CV / renal cautions

  • GI toxicity risk factors: age >65 · hx upper GI bleed · hx PUD · H. pylori · anticoagulants > antiplatelets > SSRIs/SNRIs > corticosteroids · smoking
  • Renal: avoid eGFR <60 · "triple whammy" (NSAID + ACEi/ARB + diuretic) = AKI risk
  • CV: avoid in heart failure, severe HTN, recent MI
  • Asthma: 10–15% NSAID-sensitive — caution + monitor
  • Pregnancy: avoid 3rd trimester; cautious 1st–2nd

💊 Stepwise treatment

StepAction
1 — AlwaysReassurance + stay active (bed rest >2 days worsens outcome); heat or ice; physio referral if not improving at 2 weeks; address yellow flags (psychosocial — fear of movement, catastrophising) early
2 — Paracetamol1 g PO q4–6h prn (max 4 g/24h); topical NSAID (diclofenac 1–2% gel) for localised pain
3 — Oral NSAID (short course)Choose based on patient CV/GI/renal risk (table above); with food; max 5–7 days OTC; PPI cover if GI risk factors
4 — Adjuvants (NOT in scope)Gabapentin/pregabalin (radicular), TCAs (neuropathic), muscle relaxants — refer
5 — OpioidsNOT first-line for acute MSK — refer

🗣️ Counselling (must cover)

  • Stay active — bed rest >2 days harmful for back pain
  • Heat or ice — patient preference
  • Take NSAID with food; max 5–7 days OTC; return if no improvement at 2 weeks
  • Most acute MSK resolves in 4–6 weeks
  • Avoid opioids — no functional benefit at 12 weeks; refer if patient requests

📋 Documentation

SOAP record with explicit NSAID-choice rationale (patient CV/GI/renal risk). Yellow flag acknowledgement. GP letter if Rx supplied. Retain ≥7 years.

🎯 OSCE pearls

  • Age ≥18 + mild only stated explicitly
  • Red flag screen FIRST — TUNAFISH / cauda equina explicit
  • Cauda equina: ask about saddle anaesthesia, bladder/bowel, bilateral sciatica
  • Yellow flags acknowledged
  • NSAID selection based on patient CV/GI/renal risk — narrate reasoning
  • Time-frame expectation (4–6 weeks recovery)
  • Document + GP letter

⚠️ Pitfalls

  • 55 yo male asking for "strong anti-inflammatory" with stroke hx + clopidogrel = bleeding risk → caution / refer
  • NSAID + ACEi + dehydration = AKI risk; avoid or short course + hydrate
  • 58 yo with hot swollen knee + fever 38.9°C + recent skin infection + diabetes = septic arthritis → ED, do NOT treat
  • Imaging NOT indicated for acute non-specific back pain in first 4–6 weeks unless red flag
