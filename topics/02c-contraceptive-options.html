One organising idea. Every method is just a variation on which hormone, delivered how, acting where. Oestrogen methods suppress ovulation with clean cycle control but carry the VTE & breastfeeding caveats. Progestogen-only methods either suppress ovulation (implant, DMPA) or act locally (hormonal IUD). Copper uses no hormones at all.

Match the method to the patient

The skill the OSCE rewards: anchor the choice to the patient's stated value. Pick the single best fit.

Scenario 1
Breastfeeding, wants 3–4 years before the next baby, hates needles and daily pills, squeamish about anything "up inside."
Implant. Oestrogen-free (milk-safe), not a daily pill, a rod in the arm — not a uterine insertion — and the 3-year span matches her spacing. Ticks every stated value.
Scenario 2
"I'd love fewer, lighter periods — or none at all."
Mirena. Local progestogen thins the endometrium → lighter bleeding, often amenorrhoea. Copper does the opposite (heavier).
Scenario 3
"I really don't want any hormones in my body."
Copper IUD. The only non-hormonal option — counsel that periods may get heavier/crampier as the trade-off.
Scenario 4
Wants reliable cover but is planning pregnancy in about a year; dislikes a daily routine.
Implant / LARC. Not daily and rapidly reversible. Avoid DMPA here — return to fertility can take up to ~9–12 months after the last dose.
Scenario 5
Gets migraine with aura. Wants ongoing contraception.
Avoid all oestrogen (COC & ring are UKMEC 4 with aura). Progestogen-only (POP, implant, DMPA, hormonal IUD) or copper are all fine.
Scenario 6
Unprotected sex 4 days ago, distressed about pregnancy, and wants long-term cover going forward.
Copper IUD. Within 5 days it's the most effective emergency contraception and becomes her ongoing method — two birds, one device.

Q1 · Scope
Which set can a QLD pharmacist prescribe under the pilot?
A. Four methods only. The implant and both IUDs are discuss-and-refer — but raising them (LARC-first) is still a scored action.
Q2 · Dosing
How often is DMPA (Depo) given?
12 weeks. A classic slip is "12 months" — don't make it in the station.
Q3 · Quick-start
A patient quick-starts a POP (no period to anchor to). How many days of condom backup?
2 days. The POP works via cervical mucus (dense within ~48 h). The 7-day rule is for the COC, where backup is anchored to ovulation suppression.
Q4 · Duration
Current Australian contraceptive duration for Mirena?
8 years — extended from 5 in 2024 (and usable to age 55 if inserted from 45). Kyleena stays at 5.
Q5 · Counselling
What does a copper IUD typically do to periods?
Heavier. It's hormone-free so the natural cycle continues, but the inflammatory reaction makes bleeding heavier — the main discontinuation reason.
Q6 · Effectiveness
Which is the single most effective method (fit-and-forget, <1 in 1000/yr)?
Implant. Zero user error → typical and perfect use are essentially identical. The IUDs are also tier 1.
Q7 · Fertility
Which method has a delayed return to fertility after stopping?
DMPA. The depot has a long tail — median up to ~9–12 months from the last dose. The LARCs are rapidly reversible.
Q8 · Emergency
Most effective emergency contraception (within 5 days of UPSI)?
Copper IUD. Far more effective than oral EC, and it converts straight into ongoing contraception.
