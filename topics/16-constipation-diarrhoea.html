💩 Constipation + Acute Diarrhoea

OTC + pharmacist-only management · Not currently a NSW PATH expanded scope service · Refer alarm features

🟢 Constipation

💡 Definition: infrequent stool (<3/week) OR hard/lumpy stool (Bristol 1–2) OR straining/incomplete evacuation. ≥2 of these + symptoms ≥3 months = chronic constipation. Common drug causes: opioids, anticholinergics, iron, calcium, aluminium antacids, CCBs (verapamil), diuretics, sedating antihistamines, antipsychotics, NSAIDs, anti-Parkinson, ondansetron.

💊 Stepwise management

StepAction
1 — Non-pharm (always)Fibre 25–30 g/day (gradual) · fluid 1.5–2 L/day · movement 30 min/day · toileting habits (squat position, post-meal — gastro-colic reflex)
2 — Bulk-forming ✅Psyllium (Metamucil, Fybogel) 5 g/sachet OD–TDS with water. Full glass water — oesophageal obstruction risk if dry. Slow onset (1–3 days); good long-term.
3 — Osmotic ✅Macrogol 3350 (Movicol) 1 sachet daily–TDS in 125 mL water — most effective osmotic. Lactulose (slower, bloating). Sorbitol. Magnesium hydroxide (faster, 6h).
4 — Stimulant (short-term)Senna 7.5–15 mg PRN nocte · bisacodyl 5–10 mg PO nocte OR 10 mg PR (faster). Onset 6–12h PO, 30–60 min PR. Not for daily long-term.
5 — Stool softener (adjunct)Docusate — modest effect alone; pair with stimulant (Coloxyl with senna)
6 — Rescue / refractoryGlycerol suppository PR (fast) · sodium phosphate enema (Fleet) — caution in elderly (electrolytes); avoid renal impairment · linaclotide/prucalopride (refer)

💊 Opioid-induced constipation (OIC)

  • Prevent at opioid start: stimulant + softener (senna + docusate)
  • Macrogol additive
  • Refractory: PAMORA (naloxegol, methylnaltrexone) — refer

👶 Special populations

  • Pregnancy: bulk + osmotic (lactulose, macrogol) safe; avoid prolonged stimulant; senna OK short-term
  • Children: macrogol first-line; senna OK; behavioural toileting; refer if persistent
  • Elderly: macrogol; avoid magnesium if CKD; avoid phosphate enema (electrolytes)

🟢 Acute Diarrhoea

💡 ≥3 loose/liquid stools per 24h. Acute <14 d; persistent 14–30 d; chronic >30 d. Most viral (norovirus, rotavirus, adenovirus) and self-limiting. Rehydrate first (oral rehydration solution). Anti-motility (loperamide) only if no red flags.

🔴 RED — refer urgently (ED if severe)

Trigger
Bloody diarrhoea (dysentery — Shigella, EHEC, C. difficile, IBD)
Fever ≥38.5 °C + diarrhoea
Severe abdominal pain, peritoneal signs
Severe dehydration: dry mucosa, ↓ urine output, postural drop, tachycardia, lethargy, sunken eyes (infants)
Persistent >48 h without improvement
Severe symptoms + recent overseas travel to high-risk area (parasitic, typhoid)
Recent antibiotic (consider C. difficile)
Immunocompromised, elderly, pregnant, infant, multiple comorbidities

🟠 ORANGE — TREAT (ORS / loperamide if not contraindicated) + refer GP

Trigger
Mild-moderate dehydration not improving with ORS
Chronic diarrhoea (>14 days)
Symptoms suggestive of IBD (alternating bowel, mucus, weight loss)
Loperamide contraindicated but patient distressed
Suspected drug-induced — review meds (antibiotics, metformin, magnesium, PPI causing microscopic colitis, SSRIs)
Mild stable presentation + recent overseas travel

⛔ Loperamide CONTRAINDICATED

Bloody / febrile / suspected C. difficile / IBD flare / patient <12 yo → use ORS only; refer.

🍎 Diet

  • No specific restriction — eat as tolerated
  • Bland low-fat low-fibre helpful early
  • Avoid alcohol, caffeine, high-lactose during acute

🗣️ Counselling (must cover)

  • Constipation: define clearly · medication review · fibre + fluid + movement first · time-limit stimulants
  • Diarrhoea: hydration assessment first · ORS first · loperamide contraindications stated · hand hygiene + food prep counsel

📋 Documentation

Record presenting complaint, red flag screen, drug supplied + dose, advice given. Share with GP if concerning features.

🎯 OSCE pearls

  • Constipation: define clearly; red flag screen esp. age >50 + new
  • Medication review (drugs causing)
  • Fibre + fluid + movement first
  • Diarrhoea: hydration assessment first
  • Red flag screen — blood, fever, travel, antibiotic exposure
  • ORS first; loperamide contraindications stated
  • Hand hygiene + food prep counsel

💎 Pearls

  • C. difficile — recent antibiotic (esp. clindamycin, FQ, broad-spec) + watery foul diarrhoea → stool toxin + PCR; oral vancomycin
  • Norovirus — household outbreak, hand hygiene critical (alcohol gel less effective — wash with soap/water)
  • Microscopic colitis — long-term PPI / NSAID / SSRI + chronic watery diarrhoea → refer for biopsy
  • Traveller's diarrhoea prevention: bottled water, peel-cook-boil, hand hygiene
